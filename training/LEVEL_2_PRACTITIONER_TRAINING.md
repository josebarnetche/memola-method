**Definitions:**

| Term | Definition | Example (SaaS Project Management Tool) |
|------|------------|---------------------------------------|
| **TAM** | Total global demand for the product/service category | All businesses needing project management ($50B) |
| **SAM** | Portion of TAM your business model can serve | SMBs in English-speaking markets needing cloud PM ($8B) |
| **SOM** | Realistic portion you can capture given resources | SMBs in your target verticals in next 3 years ($200M) |

### 7.2 Market Sizing Approaches

**Top-Down Approach:**

```
Start with: Industry reports, government data, analyst estimates

TAM = Industry Size × Relevant %
SAM = TAM × Serviceable %
SOM = SAM × Realistic Capture Rate

Example:
Total US marketing services market: $300B (TAM)
Digital marketing agency segment: $45B (15% of TAM = SAM starting point)
SMB clients only: $18B (40% of segment)
Your service geography: $2B (11% regional = SAM)
Realistic 3-year capture: $20M (1% of SAM = SOM)
```

**Bottom-Up Approach:**

```
Start with: Unit economics and capacity

SOM = (Target Customers × Average Revenue × Win Rate)
SAM = SOM ÷ Expected Market Share
TAM = SAM × Market Multiplier

Example:
Target customers in reach: 500 companies
Average deal size: $50,000/year
Expected win rate: 20%
SOM = 500 × $50,000 × 0.20 = $5M

Expected market share if fully captured: 2%
SAM = $5M ÷ 0.02 = $250M

SAM represents 30% of total market
TAM = $250M ÷ 0.30 = $833M
```

**Value-Theory Approach:**

```
Start with: Customer problem value

TAM = (Customers with Problem) × (Value of Solution to Each)

Example:
Businesses losing money to marketing waste: 2M companies
Average annual marketing waste: $50,000
Value of solving (50% waste reduction): $25,000
TAM = 2M × $25,000 = $50B
```

### 7.3 Market Share Calculation

**Market Share Types:**

| Type | Formula | Use Case |
|------|---------|----------|
| Revenue Share | Company Revenue / Market Revenue | Overall market position |
| Unit Share | Company Units / Market Units | Volume-based businesses |
| Customer Share | Company Customers / Market Customers | Subscription businesses |
| Relative Share | Company Share / Largest Competitor Share | Competitive positioning |

### 8.3 Marketing Mix Optimization

**Channel Portfolio Analysis:**

```
                    HIGH EFFICIENCY (Low CAC, High ROAS)
                              ▲
                              │
              INVEST          │         OPTIMIZE
         (Scale Spending)     │    (Maintain Efficiency)
                              │
   LOW ◄──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────► HIGH
 VOLUME                       │                          VOLUME
                              │
             TEST             │         EVALUATE
        (Small Bets)          │    (Improve or Exit)
                              │
                              ▼
                    LOW EFFICIENCY (High CAC, Low ROAS)


QUADRANT STRATEGIES:

INVEST (High Efficiency, Low Volume)
• Increase budget incrementally (20-30% per period)
• Monitor for efficiency degradation
• Goal: Maximize volume while maintaining efficiency

OPTIMIZE (High Efficiency, High Volume)
• Protect current performance
• Test incremental improvements
• Expand to adjacent audiences
• Goal: Maintain efficiency, grow slightly

EVALUATE (Low Efficiency, High Volume)
• Deep-dive into performance drivers
• Test aggressive optimization
• Set efficiency targets and timeline
• Goal: Improve to target or reallocate

TEST (Low Efficiency, Low Volume)
• Run structured experiments
• Set clear success criteria
• Quick decision cycle (30-60 days)
• Goal: Find path to efficiency or exit
```

### 8.4 Budget Allocation Modeling

**The MEMOLA Budget Allocation Model:**

**Step 1: Establish Baseline**
```
Current Budget Allocation:
├── Channel A: 40% ($40,000/mo)
├── Channel B: 25% ($25,000/mo)
├── Channel C: 20% ($20,000/mo)
├── Channel D: 10% ($10,000/mo)
└── Channel E: 5% ($5,000/mo)

Current Performance:
├── Channel A: CAC $120, ROAS 2.5x
├── Channel B: CAC $80, ROAS 4.2x
├── Channel C: CAC $200, ROAS 1.8x
├── Channel D: CAC $95, ROAS 3.1x
└── Channel E: CAC $150, ROAS 2.0x
```

**Step 2: Apply Efficiency Ranking**
```
Efficiency Score = (1/CAC) × ROAS × 1000

Channel B: (1/80) × 4.2 × 1000 = 52.5  ★ Best
Channel D: (1/95) × 3.1 × 1000 = 32.6
Channel A: (1/120) × 2.5 × 1000 = 20.8
Channel E: (1/150) × 2.0 × 1000 = 13.3
Channel C: (1/200) × 1.8 × 1000 = 9.0   ★ Worst
```

**Step 3: Model Reallocation Scenarios**
```
Scenario 1: Efficiency-Weighted Allocation
├── Channel B: 35% (+10%) → Watch for diminishing returns
├── Channel D: 25% (+15%) → Scale test
├── Channel A: 25% (-15%) → Maintain core
├── Channel E: 10% (+5%)  → Test expansion
└── Channel C: 5% (-15%)  → Reduce, evaluate exit

Scenario 2: Aggressive Optimization
├── Channel B: 45% → Maximum efficient channel
├── Channel D: 30% → Second priority
├── Channel A: 20% → Reduced to essential
├── Channel E: 5%  → Maintain test
└── Channel C: 0%  → Exit

Scenario 3: Balanced Growth
├── Channel B: 30% → Scale with monitoring
├── Channel D: 20% → Grow sustainably
├── Channel A: 30% → Maintain strong presence
├── Channel E: 10% → Expand test
└── Channel C: 10% → Optimization period
```

# MODULE 9: Strategy Development and Recommendations

### 9.1 The Strategic Recommendation Framework

**SMART-C Recommendations:**

Every strategic recommendation must be:

```
S - SPECIFIC
    Not: "Improve paid social performance"
    But: "Reduce Meta Ads CAC from $125 to $95 through
         audience refinement and creative testing"

M - MEASURABLE
    Include: Target metric, current baseline, target value
    Example: "Increase email-attributed revenue from $72K
             to $100K monthly"

A - ACTIONABLE
    Include: Specific activities to execute
    Example: "Implement A/B testing for email subject lines,
             increase send frequency from 2x to 3x monthly,
             add automated welcome sequence"

R - RESOURCED
    Include: Budget, time, people required
    Example: "Requires $5K additional email tool investment,
             20 hours/month content creation,
             dedicated email specialist"

T - TIMEBOUND
    Include: Start date, milestones, end date
    Example: "Launch improved program by March 1,
             mid-point review April 15,
             target achievement by June 1"

C - CONNECTED
    Include: Link to Phase 1-3 insights
    Example: "Based on Phase 1 finding that email delivers
             7.2x ROAS (highest efficiency) and Phase 2
             competitive gap in email marketing"
```

### 10.1 The Co-Production Imperative

**Why Workshops Matter in MEMOLA:**

The servuction model places the client as co-producer. Strategy workshops are the primary mechanism for strategic co-creation, ensuring:

1. **Client Ownership** - Strategies developed with clients are implemented by clients
2. **Insight Integration** - Client knowledge combines with analytical findings
3. **Alignment Building** - Multiple stakeholders reach shared understanding
4. **Reality Testing** - Strategic ideas validated against operational constraints

### 10.2 Workshop Types and Purposes

**MEMOLA Workshop Catalog:**

| Workshop | Purpose | Participants | Duration | Phase |
|----------|---------|--------------|----------|-------|
| Discovery | Gather client context, goals, constraints | Leadership + Marketing | 2 hours | Pre-Phase 1 |

### 10.4 Facilitation Techniques

**The Facilitator's Role:**

As MEMOLA Practitioner, you facilitate—not dictate:

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Ask open questions | Provide all the answers |
| Summarize and synthesize | Impose your conclusions |
| Ensure all voices heard | Let dominant voices monopolize |
| Keep on time and topic | Allow endless tangents |
| Document decisions visibly | Make decisions for the group |
| Surface disagreements | Suppress conflict |

**Key Facilitation Techniques:**

**1. The Parking Lot**
```
"That's an important point, but outside today's scope.
Let me add it to our parking lot—we'll address it
in our next session or a separate discussion."
```

**2. The Diplomatic Redirect**
```
"I appreciate that perspective. I'd love to hear from
[quieter participant]—what's your view on this?"
```

**3. The Synthesis Check**
```
"Let me make sure I'm capturing this correctly.
The group is suggesting [summary]. Is that accurate?
What am I missing?"
```

**4. The Decision Push**
```
"We've had good discussion. Now we need to decide.
Given what we've heard, what's our direction?
I'm seeing three options: [A], [B], or [C].
Can we get to a decision?"
```

**5. The Constraint Reality Check**
```
"That's an ambitious goal. What would need to be true
for that to work? What obstacles do you see?"
```
